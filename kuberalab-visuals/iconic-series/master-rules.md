# ICONIC SERIES — Master Rules

## Visual identity

1. The machine remains the hero. Do not replace its real silhouette with a generic fantasy object.
2. Preserve recognisable geometry, proportions, materials and defining details from the supplied reference.
3. Add mythology through atmosphere, scale, light, environment and motion — not by destroying product identity.
4. Keep the Saturn seal restrained. It is a signature, not the main subject.
5. Prefer one dominant visual idea per frame.

## Series language

- **ARMORED POWER** — protection, force, presence.
- **MEGA YACHTS** — ocean, scale, silence.
- **MEGA TRACTORS** — earth, torque, dominion.
- **E-MOUNTAIN BIKES** — voltage, terrain, motion.

## Accuracy boundary

- Do not invent technical specifications, prices, model names, performance figures or manufacturer claims.
- Do not imply sponsorship, partnership or endorsement unless it is real and supplied.
- If a brand/model is uncertain, describe the visible object rather than guessing.
- Logos and text should remain faithful when legible in the source; do not fabricate unreadable branding.

## Image quality

- No duplicate machine, ghost copy, warped wheels, broken geometry or impossible reflections.
- No accidental extra parts, floating components or deformed bodywork.
- Materials must stay coherent: metal behaves like metal, glass like glass, rubber like rubber.
- Lighting should explain the form rather than hide defects.

## Motion quality

- Motion begins from the established world, not from an unrelated title card.
- Camera movement must have a clear purpose: reveal scale, speed, surface or environment.
- Avoid random transformations and morphing unless the concept explicitly requires them.
- Keep short-form motion legible on a phone screen.
